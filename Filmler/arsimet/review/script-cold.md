# Cold read: script-lines.txt (VO1-VO92)

Read only review/script-lines.txt, as a first-time viewer.

## 1. What happens, in order

1. A narrator lists three famous Archimedes stories (mirrors, bath, lever) and asks which really happened (VO1-VO4).
2. The rules: seven claims, three possible verdicts, the viewer guesses first. The best story is saved for last and takes place in Istanbul (VO5-VO9).
3. Round 1, the crown and the bath: Vitruvius wrote it about 200 years later. The buoyancy science is real. Kısmen doğru (VO10-VO21).
4. Round 2, burning mirrors at the siege of 214 BC: the earliest historians say nothing, and the 2005 MIT test only worked on a ship that stayed still. Efsane (VO22-VO32).
5. Round 3, the crane with the iron claw: the sources agree, and in 2005 engineers built a copy that tipped over a model ship. Doğru (VO33-VO42).
6. Round 4, "give me a place to stand": he really did prove the lever law, but Pappus reported the sentence 500 years later. Kısmen doğru (VO43-VO52).
7. Round 5, the Archimedes screw: Vitruvius does not name him and the Egyptians may already have used it. Kısmen doğru (VO53-VO59).
8. Round 6, his death in 212 BC and "don't disturb my circles": a soldier did kill him, but the sentence is in no ancient source. Kısmen doğru (VO60-VO70).
9. Cicero finds the tomb, which has a sphere and a cylinder on it, about 140 years later (VO71-VO73).
10. Final round: in 1229 a book by Archimedes is scraped off and prayers are written over it. Heiberg finds it in Istanbul in 1906. It disappears, sells in New York in 1998, is scanned and shows a method that came before calculus. Doğru (VO74-VO89).
11. A call to post your score, and a closing line: the most-told stories were the least reliable, and the truest one was hidden under prayers (VO90-VO92).

## 2. Places I had to guess

### Referents (a person, thing or term never introduced)
- VO15 "Bu hikâye": is it the bath-and-run or the crown test? The verdict in VO21 covers both, but the question does not say which.
- VO18 "Galileo ise ... kullandığını düşünüyordu": Galileo appears with no introduction. The "ise" contrast is with nothing the viewer heard, and "kullandığını" has no stated subject (Archimedes, presumably).
- VO23 "askerlere": whose soldiers? Siracusa's, presumably. Up to this point we only know of Romans attacking.
- VO26 vs VO38: VO26 says "en eski üç tarihçi" without names. VO38 names three. Are they the same three? If so, VO37's claim that the sources agree "this time" rests on the same people who stayed silent about the mirrors, and this is never connected.
- VO30 "Gemi": a test ship that was never introduced.
- VO38 "bu makineleri": plural, but only one crane was described.
- VO40 "mühendisler": unnamed. This is the second "2005" in a row (after VO29), so a listener can easily merge it with MIT.
- VO41 "gemi maketini": VO40 "bir benzerini" does not say what scale. Was it a full-size claw tipping a model ship? The verdict rests on this.
- VO47 "Kol uzadıkça": heard once, "kol" sounds like a human arm. "Kaldıraç kolu" is never set up.
- VO51 "bu ayrıntılara": which details? The pulleys, the ship, or Plutarch's account as a whole?
- VO68 "En yakını": the nearest version, written by whom? The name appears only as an on-screen tag (Valerius Maximus), never in the VO.
- VO69 "bunu": the drawing, I assume.
- VO73 "Arşimet'in en değer verdiği buluş": which discovery? A sphere and a cylinder are shown, but their relationship (the discovery itself) is never stated. The line is also a fragment.
- VO79 "Bu kitap": the prayer book or Archimedes' lost book? They are the same object, but that is only implied.
- VO87 "İçinde": inside what? The book, I assume.
- VO88 "Kalkülüs": the term is never introduced. Most viewers will know it, but "sonsuz küçük parçalarla hesap yapan bir yöntem" (VO87) does not explain it either.
- VO90 "Skorunu": scoring was never set up. VO7 only says you guess.
- On screen: "Galen" and the year "2008" appear as tags but not in the VO, so a viewer without sound context cannot place them.

### Causes never given
- VO12: why the crown must not be melted is never said.
- VO13-VO14: the link between the water overflowing and solving the crown problem is never stated. The viewer must already know the trick. The crown's outcome (did the goldsmith cheat?) is never answered.
- VO19: buoyancy is presented as the "real" part, but how it connects to the crown is not given.
- VO22: why Rome attacks Siracusa is not given (minor).
- VO60: how the city falls after the claw defense "worked" is not given.
- VO66: Marcellus ordered that Archimedes be spared (VO61), yet a soldier kills him. No reason is given, and the killing is first stated inside a verdict line, not in the story.
- VO71: why Cicero searches for the tomb is not given.
- VO75-VO77: who scrapes and washes the pages, and why parchment was reused, are not given (passive voice throughout).
- VO83: why and where the book disappears, and whose cellar it was, are not given.
- VO84-VO85: who bought it and who scanned it are not given.

### Jumps in time or place
- VO9 promises Istanbul, but the final story opens in VO75 with "1229, Kudüs". The viewer wonders whether this is the promised story. How the book got from Kudüs to Istanbul is never said.
- VO10: the crown story has no date, while the next rounds have dates (214, 212).
- VO17, VO28, VO49, VO68, VO71: the anchors "X yıl sonra" are counted from his death in some lines (VO17) and from the siege or his lifetime in others (VO28, VO49). His death date (VO60) comes after most of them.
- VO79: the question asks if it was "found in Istanbul", but this was never stated as a claim. VO9 only said the story "takes place" there.
- VO83 to VO84: from 1906 to a cellar to New York in 1998, with no bridge.

### Unclear when heard once
- VO36 "Bu da mı fazla abartılı?": this question is inverted compared with the others ("doğru mu?"). The answer "Doğru." (VO42) then reads as "yes, exaggerated", which is the opposite of what is meant.
- VO39 "Yani aşağı yukarı kapkaççı": right after a crane that lifts and drops a ship, "aşağı yukarı" is heard as "up and down", not "roughly".
- VO2 / VO3: these are elliptical ("...da.") and lean on "anlatılır" from VO1. They work in print but are fragile by ear.
- VO52 "Fikir onun, cümle sonradan.": this is compressed. "cümle sonradan" has no verb.
- VO74 "Ve sona sakladığım hikâye.": this fragment follows the tomb with no round or verdict for Cicero, so it is unclear whether the tomb was the story or a bridge.
- VO91: it says the most-told stories were the "least reliable", but only one of seven was "Efsane". Most were "Kısmen doğru", so the summary does not match what the viewer just scored.

### Turkish that sounds unnatural or translated
- VO19 "suyun cisimleri kaldırdığını": this is understandable, but "suyun cisimlere kaldırma kuvveti uyguladığını" is the usual phrasing. As written it sounds like a literal translation.
- VO24 "Güneşi tek noktaya toplarlar": they gather the sun's light, not the sun.
- VO36 "fazla abartılı": this is redundant.
- VO53 / VO57: "burgu" and "vida" are mixed for the same object.
- VO62 "kuma geometri çizmektedir": geometry itself is not drawn. The phrase is a calque.
- VO87 "sonsuz küçük parçalarla hesap yapan bir yöntem": this is clunky.
- VO89 "Hem de hepsinden doğru": "truer than all" does not fit a three-value verdict scale and sounds odd.
- VO91 "en az güvenilenleri çıktı": this sounds stiff and translated.
